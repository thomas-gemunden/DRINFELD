# Detecting-family literature sources for H^3(E_+(m); U(1))

**Date**: 2026-05-17
**Issue**: #193
**Status**: complete

## Summary

No single source in the literature states the claim "res: H^3(E_+(m); U(1)) → H^3(E) ⊕ H^3(D_8) ⊕ H^3(Q_8) is injective for m ≥ 2" in exactly that form. The claim is, however, a routine assembly of three published results:

1. **Quillen 1971 (Theorem 5.10)** — H*(E_+(m); F_2) injects into the product of H*(M^∼; F_2) over the maximal elementary abelian subgroups M^∼ ⊂ E_+(m). This is the mod-2 backbone.
2. **Harada–Kono 1987 (Theorem 1.1)** — pinpoints the *cyclic* summand A(k) of H^k(E_+(m); Z) and shows it is of order 2^a(k,Q) for k ≡ 0 mod 4 (zero otherwise); for k = 4 (= H^3 with U(1) coefficients) it equals Z/8 at m = 2 and Z/4 at m ≥ 3. This identifies the obstruction Quillen's mod-2 theorem does not see.
3. **Pham 2000 / Adem–Karagueuzian 1997** — Q_8 is the unique 2-group whose essential cohomology Ess(G) is non-zero (it is the unique 2-group in which every element of order 2 is central). This is the structural reason Q_8 cannot be replaced by a smaller section.

The cleanest citation chain is **Quillen 1971 + Harada–Kono 1987 + UCT**, with Adem–Milgram 2004 as the textbook reference for the framework. The conclusion that Q_8 specifically (rather than e.g. another rank-2 section) is required to catch the cyclic A(4) summand is empirical (#151) and consistent with — but not literally stated by — Adem–Karagueuzian / Pham. **Verdict: B** (see below).

## The claim

For E_+(m) the *real* extraspecial 2-group of order 2^(2m+1) (a central Z/2 extension of (Z/2)^(2m) with Arf invariant 0), and for m ≥ 2, the restriction map

```
res : H^3(E_+(m); U(1)) → H^3(E; U(1)) ⊕ H^3(D_8; U(1)) ⊕ H^3(Q_8; U(1))
```

is injective. Here:
- E is a maximal elementary abelian subgroup of E_+(m), of rank m+1 (the inverse image of a maximal Q-isotropic subspace);
- D_8 is a dihedral section of order 8 (any of the rank-2 real-type extraspecial subgroups containing the centre);
- Q_8 is a quaternion section of order 8 (a rank-2 quaternion-type extraspecial subgroup containing the centre). For m ≥ 2 such a Q_8 always exists, as E_+(m) ≅ D_8 * Q_8 * (m−2 copies of D_8) by Adem–Milgram Remark IV.2.13.

The U(1) coefficient is the relevant one for twisted Drinfeld doubles / Dijkgraaf–Witten: H^3(G; U(1)) classifies 3-cocycles up to coboundary. By the Universal Coefficient Theorem applied to 0 → Z → R → U(1) → 0 (and the fact that H^k(BG; R) = 0 for k > 0, G finite), we have a natural isomorphism H^3(G; U(1)) ≅ H^4(G; Z), and this isomorphism is compatible with restriction.

## Sources

### Quillen 1971

D. Quillen, "The Mod 2 Cohomology Rings of Extra-special 2-groups and the Spinor Groups", *Mathematische Annalen* 194, 197–212 (1971).

File: `docs/Quillen - The mod 2 cohomology of extra-special 2-groups and the spinor groups.pdf` (in the main DRINFELD repo, not the r1 worktree).

- **Relevance to our claim**: Provides the mod-2 detection theorem from which everything else is bootstrapped via UCT/Bockstein. Establishes that the extraspecial-2-group cohomology ring is "detected" by *elementary abelians only* at mod-2 coefficients.
- **Key theorems**:
  - **Theorem 4.6** (p. 205): H*(BṼ; F_2) ≅ S(V*)/J ⊗_{F_2} F_2[w_{2^h}] where J is the ideal generated by the regular sequence Q(x), B(x, x^2), …, B(x, x^{2^{h-1}}). Here Ṽ = π^{-1}(W^⊥) where π: Ṽ → V is the central extension, and W is a maximal Q-isotropic subspace.
  - **Theorem 5.10** (p. 208): "The homomorphism H*(BṼ) → ∏_W H*(B(W^⊥)^∼) is injective, where the product is taken over all maximal Q-isotropic subspaces W of V." Here (W^⊥)^∼ is exactly the maximal elementary abelian subgroup of Ṽ corresponding to W.
- **Coefficients**: F_2. The theorem is purely mod-2.
- **Caveats**:
  - The theorem covers **all** central Z/2-extensions of elementary abelian 2-groups by quadratic functions Q: V → F_2, not just E_+(m). It includes E_+(m) (real type, codim h = m), E_-(m) (quaternion type, h = m+1), and complex-type analogues.
  - The codimension parameter h satisfies h = m for the real case (V^- = E_+(m)) and h = m+1 for the quaternion case (V^+ = E_-(m)).
  - **Does not directly imply the U(1) / integer-coefficient case.** The injectivity at F_2 lifts to injectivity at Z only modulo the cyclic A(k) summand, which Quillen does not see.
  - For m = 1 (i.e. Ṽ = D_8): the theorem still applies and reduces to D_8's well-known decomposition H*(D_8; F_2) = F_2[x, y, w]/(xy) (cf. Adem–Milgram Theorem IV.2.7). For m = 1 quaternion (Ṽ = Q_8): the maximal elementary abelians are *cyclic of order 2* (Z(Q_8) only), so Quillen's detection on elementary abelians **collapses** at Q_8 — exactly the source of Q_8's essential cohomology.

### Adem–Milgram 2004

A. Adem and R. J. Milgram, *Cohomology of Finite Groups*, 2nd edition, *Grundlehren der mathematischen Wissenschaften* **309**, Springer (2004).

File: `docs/bok%3A978-3-662-06280-7.pdf`.

- **Relevance to our claim**: Standard textbook reference for the framework: detection theorems, the Quillen–Venkov F-isomorphism, explicit cohomology rings for D_{2^n} and Q_{2^n}. The book does **not** state the exact {E, D_8, Q_8} injectivity in the form we use it.
- **Key theorems / sections**:
  - **§IV.2 Theorem 2.7** (p. 127): H*(D_{2^n}; F_2) = F_2[x, y, w]/(xy) with deg x = deg y = 1, deg w = 2, and Sq^1(w) = (x + y)w. For D_8 the cohomology is fully captured by restriction to the two Klein-4 subgroups ⟨x⟩ × ⟨centre⟩ and ⟨y⟩ × ⟨centre⟩ — i.e. D_8 is *itself* detected by elementary abelians at mod-2.
  - **§IV.2 Theorem 2.9, Lemma 2.10** (p. 129): H*(Q_8; F_2) = F_2[x, y, e_4]/(x^2 + xy + y^2, x^2 y + xy^2) where deg x = deg y = 1, deg e_4 = 4, and Q_8 is periodic of period 4. The degree-4 generator e_4 is **not** in the image of restriction from any proper subgroup — this is the explicit essential class.
  - **§IV.4 Theorem 4.3** (p. 137, due to Quillen [Q6], anticipated by Nakaoka): If H*(G; F_p) is detected by abelian subgroups, then so is H*(G ≀ Z/p; F_p). The *definition* of "detected by abelian subgroups" (Definition 4.2) is precisely an injectivity statement of the form we want, restricted to F_p coefficients.
  - **§IV.5 Theorem 5.6** (p. 142, the Quillen F-isomorphism): The map H*(G; F_p) → lim_{A ∈ A_p(G)} H*(A; F_p) has **nilpotent** kernel and cokernel. So the kernel of restriction to elementary abelians is concentrated in nilpotent classes — but is not necessarily zero. The F-isomorphism does not directly give injectivity in any single degree, only after taking quotients by nilpotents.
  - **§IV.2 Remark 2.13** (p. 130): Every extraspecial 2-group is a central product D_8 ∗ ⋯ ∗ D_8 ∗ Q_8 ∗ ⋯ ∗ Q_8 ∗ (possibly Z/4). For E_+(m) the product is D_8^{∗m} (real type) or D_8^{∗(m−1)} ∗ Q_8 (quaternion type m = 1 only gives quaternion exactly). **For m ≥ 2 real type, the structure is iterated D_8 central products** — D_8 ∗ D_8 ≅ Q_8 ∗ Q_8 means E_+(m) for m ≥ 2 does contain Q_8 as a section, via the standard isomorphism.
- **Coefficients**: All major detection results in AM04 IV.4–IV.5 are stated for F_p; the integral case is not the focus.
- **Caveats**:
  - The codebase's existing citation "AM04 IV.7" appears to be a slip — IV.7 in the 2nd edition is the *definition of Steenrod squares*, not a detection theorem. The intended reference is presumably IV.4 ("Groups with Cohomology Detected by Abelian Subgroups") or IV.5 (the F-isomorphism). **Fix this citation in the methodology paper.**
  - AM04 does **not** state or prove a {E, D_8, Q_8} integral detection theorem. The reader has to assemble Quillen 1971 + Harada–Kono 1987 + UCT themselves; AM04 supplies only the F_2 ingredients and the D_8 / Q_8 cohomology computations.

### Pham 2000

Pham Anh Minh, "Essential Cohomology and Extraspecial p-Groups", *Transactions of the American Mathematical Society* **353** (5), 1937–1957 (2000).

File: `docs/S0002-9947-00-02689-1.pdf`.

- **Relevance to our claim**: Provides the structural justification for *why* Q_8 has to appear in any detecting family at p = 2. Specifically: Q_8 is the **only** p = 2 extraspecial group with non-zero essential cohomology, and any 2-group containing Q_8 inherits classes that *every* maximal subgroup fails to see.
- **Key theorem**: (Introduction, p. 1937) "For p = 2, it was proved by Quillen ([17]) that H*(G) is Cohen-Macaulay and Ess(G) = {0}, except for the case G = Q_8, the quaternion group of order 8 (this fact also follows from Adem and Karagueuzian's result, as Q_8 is the unique group in which every element of order 2 is central)."
  - Pham's main theorem (§1, p. 1937) is about odd primes p ≥ 3, but the p = 2 statement is quoted as a known result attributed to Quillen 1971.
- **Coefficients**: F_p (here F_2 in the relevant subcase). The result is a statement about the kernel of restriction to *all proper subgroups*; it does **not** address restriction to specific dihedral / quaternion sections in a positive group.
- **Caveats**:
  - "Ess(G) = 0 for G = E_+(m), m ≥ 2" means *every* mod-2 cohomology class of E_+(m) restricts non-trivially to *some* proper subgroup. But it does **not** tell us *which* subgroups suffice — Quillen 1971 Theorem 5.10 supplies that for elementary abelians at mod-2.
  - The interaction with the cyclic A(4) summand (which is integral, not mod-2) is **not** discussed in Pham. Pham is purely mod-p.
  - The fact "Q_8 has non-zero essential cohomology" is the key structural reason Q_8 cannot be detected by its own elementary abelians. But for our setup E_+(m) with m ≥ 2 *contains* Q_8 as a section, so we add Q_8 to the detecting family to pick up classes that Q_8's essential cohomology would otherwise hide if Q_8 were the ambient group. This is a different direction of use than Pham's statement, but the structural fact is the same.

### Harada–Kono 1987

M. Harada and A. Kono, "On the integral cohomology of extraspecial 2-groups", *Journal of Pure and Applied Algebra* **44**, 215–219 (1987).

File: `docs/1-s2.0-0022404987900259-main.pdf`.

- **Relevance to our claim**: **This is the closest direct citation.** Pinpoints the cyclic summand A(k) of H^k(V^-; Z) — exactly the structure Quillen's mod-2 theorem misses, and exactly the structure Q_8 catches via its degree-4 essential class e_4 ∈ H^4(Q_8; F_2) (which lifts to a generator of Z/8 ⊂ H^4(Q_8; Z)).
- **Key theorem**:
  - **Theorem 1.1** (p. 215): "If h(Q) ≥ 2, then the abelian group A(k) is a cyclic group of order 2^a(k, Q) if k ≡ 0 mod 4 and is equal to zero if k ≢ 0 mod 4." Here h(Q) is the codimension of a maximal Q-isotropic subspace (in our notation, h = m for E_+(m) real type; the condition h ≥ 2 corresponds to m ≥ 2).
  - **The formula for a(k, Q)** (p. 215, top): a(k, Q) = n(k) if n(k) ≤ h(Q) − 1, else a(k, Q) = h(Q) + 1, where n(k) is the 2-adic valuation of k.
  - **Concrete values at k = 4 (= H^3 with U(1) coefficients)**: n(4) = 2.
    - For D_8 / Q_8 (h(Q) = 1 by convention, handled separately in the introduction): ~~A(4) = Z/8 directly.~~ **[CORRECTED in R-1 follow-up, see #215 / PR #219.]** Only Q_8 has H^4(Q_8; Z) ≅ Z/8 (cyclic of order |Q_8| by 4-periodicity, AM04 §IV.2 Thm 2.9). For D_8, H^4(D_8; Z) ≅ Z/2 × Z/2 × Z/4 — its **maximum cyclic factor is Z/4, not Z/8** (centre Z/2, abelianisation (Z/2)², so no central Z/4 quotient). See `2026-05-17-d8-vs-q8-detection.md` for the HAP-verified torsion structure.
    - For E_+(m) m = 2 (h = 2): n(k) = 2 > h − 1 = 1, so a = h + 1 = 3 → **A(4) = Z/8**.
    - For E_+(m) m ≥ 3 (h ≥ 3): n(k) = 2 ≤ h − 1, so a = n(k) = 2 → **A(4) = Z/4**.
  - **Theorem 2.5** (p. 218): Sq^1-cohomology structure of H*(V^-; F_2) — this is the technical input that lifts to the integral computation via the Bockstein spectral sequence.
- **Coefficients**: Z (integral). This is the only one of the four sources that directly computes integral cohomology.
- **Caveats**:
  - Theorem 1.1 covers the full mod-{2 torsion} integral cohomology, of which the cyclic A(k) is just one summand. The other summand is "an elementary abelian 2-group" — exactly what Quillen 1971 captures via mod-2 detection.
  - The Harada–Kono notation: their V^- is *real-type* extraspecial 2-group (= our E_+(m)), and their V^+ is *quaternion-type* (= our E_-(m)). The codimension h is one less for real type than for quaternion type at fixed order. **Sanity check**: at m = 2, h(Q) = 2 for E_+(2) (order 32) — consistent with the formula giving A(4) = Z/8, which matches our empirical observation in the issue.
  - Theorem 1.1 explicitly **excludes** h(Q) ≤ 1 (i.e. excludes D_8 and Q_8 themselves). For those the integral cohomology was already classical (D_8: ~~A(4) = Z/8 from the central Z/4 piece~~ **H^4(D_8; Z) ≅ Z/2 × Z/2 × Z/4** with maximum cyclic factor Z/4 — D_8 has centre Z/2 only, no central Z/4 quotient — plus the Klein-4 elementary abelian residue [corrected per R-1 follow-up #215 / PR #219]; Q_8: H^4(Q_8; Z) ≅ Z/8 generated by e_4).
  - Does **not** directly state our injectivity claim. But by combining with Quillen 1971 Theorem 5.10 the picture is complete: Quillen catches everything in the elementary-abelian 2-part of H^*(E_+(m); Z), Harada–Kono identifies the residual cyclic part A(4) which is *exactly* the obstruction to Quillen-only detection, and Q_8's degree-4 essential class is what surjects onto A(4) under restriction.

## Verdict

**Verdict B**: No single source states the U(1) / degree-3 / extraspecial-2-group injectivity claim in our exact form. The claim follows from the synthesis below, with the principal three citations being **Quillen 1971 (Theorem 5.10) + Harada–Kono 1987 (Theorem 1.1) + UCT**.

### Synthesis (the proof, in W-1 form)

Let G = E_+(m), m ≥ 2. We want: ker(res: H^3(G; U(1)) → H^3(E; U(1)) ⊕ H^3(D_8; U(1)) ⊕ H^3(Q_8; U(1))) = 0.

**Step 1: UCT reduction.** Apply 0 → Z → R → U(1) → 0 and the fact H^k(BG; R) = 0 for finite G, k ≥ 1, to obtain a natural isomorphism

> δ: H^3(G; U(1)) → H^4(G; Z), compatible with restriction along any subgroup inclusion.

So injectivity on H^3(−; U(1)) is equivalent to injectivity on H^4(−; Z).

**Step 2: Decompose H^4(G; Z).** By Harada–Kono 1987 Theorem 1.1, H^4(G; Z) ≅ A(4) ⊕ V where:
- A(4) is cyclic: Z/8 at m = 2, Z/4 at m ≥ 3 (using a(4, Q) per Harada–Kono).
- V is elementary abelian 2-torsion.

**Step 3: Detect V on the family of maximal elementary abelians.** By Quillen 1971 Theorem 5.10, the restriction H*(G; F_2) → ∏_M H*(M^∼; F_2) is injective, where M^∼ ranges over **all** maximal elementary abelian subgroups of G. The elementary abelian 2-torsion summand V of H^4(G; Z) sits inside H^4(G; F_2) (via the integral-to-mod-2 reduction Z/2 → F_2), and restriction is a map of integral cohomology lattices; the F_2 injectivity therefore gives integral injectivity on the elementary abelian summand. The full family at E_+(m) has size N_+(m, 2) = ∏_{i=0}^{m-1}(2^i + 1) = 6, 30, 270, 4590, 75582, 9 845 550 at m = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7. **CORRECTION** (per #289 audit, fixed in #291): an earlier draft of this section claimed that "for E_+(m) every maximal elementary abelian is conjugate to a single one in Aut(G), so the restriction to one E is injective on V modulo Aut(G)". That argument was wrong. Aut(G) is indeed transitive on type-+ Lagrangians for m ≥ 2 (so the family forms a single Aut(G)-orbit at the subgroup level), but Aut-equivariance acts on **cocycles modulo coboundaries**, not on **subgroups**. The kernel of res to a single maximal elementary abelian is generally non-trivial, and Aut(G) sends it to ker(res) for another maximal elementary abelian, not back to itself. Empirically refuted at m=2 (generators 1 and 5 of H^3(E_+(2); U(1)) have zero restriction to a single max EA but are detected by other max EAs). The audit research note `docs/superpowers/research/2026-05-19-d-van-detecting-family-correctness.md` records the full reconciliation.

**Step 4: Detect A(4) on D_8 ∪ Q_8.** The cyclic summand A(4) ⊂ H^4(G; Z) is *not* detected by E (its image vanishes in H^4(E; Z) ≅ ⊕ Z/2 for E elementary abelian rank m+1). To detect A(4):
- For m = 2: A(4) = Z/8, and we restrict to Q_8 ⊂ E_+(2). H^4(Q_8; Z) ≅ Z/8 (= A(4) of Q_8 itself, generated by e_4). The restriction map A(4)(E_+(2)) → A(4)(Q_8) is non-zero (both sides Z/8, both generated by the unique class detected by the central Z/4 quotient action — see Adem–Milgram §IV.2 Lemma 2.10). Injectivity at this single class follows from the fact that the central Z/4 of Q_8 sits inside the central Z/4 of E_+(2) (well, the centre is Z/2; the relevant Z/4 is the cyclic quotient ⟨a⟩ in Q_8 = ⟨a, b | a^4 = 1, b^2 = a^2, bab^{-1} = a^{-1}⟩). **TODO for the methodology paper**: write out this restriction explicitly — it is morally Brumfield–Morgan's Z/4-Hirzebruch / Pontryagin-square trick.
- For m ≥ 3: A(4) = Z/4, and we restrict to Q_8 ⊂ E_+(m) (which exists since D_8 ∗ D_8 ≅ Q_8 ∗ Q_8, so any E_+(m) for m ≥ 2 contains Q_8 as a section). The Z/4 ⊂ Q_8's Z/8 = H^4(Q_8; Z) is detected by the same restriction argument.

For D_8: ~~H^4(D_8; Z) also contains a Z/8 generated by the central Z/4 of D_8, but the cyclic summand of E_+(m) restricts to this *as well* — both D_8 and Q_8 catch A(4). In practice we could omit one of them, but for the W-1 paper we keep both because:~~ **[REFUTED in R-1 follow-up, see #215 / PR #219.]** Corrected: H^4(D_8; Z) ≅ Z/2 × Z/2 × Z/4 has maximum cyclic factor Z/4 (not Z/8); D_8 has centre Z/2 with abelianisation (Z/2)², so there is no "central Z/4 quotient" giving a Z/8 in H^4. The restriction A(4) → D_8 lands on the Z/4 factor via multiplication by 2, missing the order-2 sub-element (verified by exhaustive scan of all 280 D_8 conjugacy classes in E_+(3)). **Q_8 is therefore mathematically required for A(4) detection — D_8 alone does not suffice at any m ≥ 2.** We keep both because:
(a) D_8 is the "natural" choice from AM04 IV.4 for the F_2 / elementary-abelian side (it is itself detected by elementary abelians at F_2 by Theorem 2.7);
(b) Q_8 is *both* structurally necessary by Pham 2000 / Adem–Karagueuzian (Q_8 has non-zero essential cohomology) *and* the unique 2-group of order 8 whose H^4(−; Z) carries a Z/8 — i.e., Q_8 is the only rank-2 section that can faithfully detect the cyclic A(4) summand. Empirically confirmed in #151 and resolved in R-1 follow-up `2026-05-17-d8-vs-q8-detection.md` / PR #219;
(c) The two are **complementary, not redundant**: D_8 contributes F_2-side coverage and partial Z/4-coverage of A(4); Q_8 contributes the order-2-of-A(4) detection that D_8 structurally cannot.

**Step 5: Combine.** Steps 3 and 4 cover the two summands of H^4(G; Z) given by Harada–Kono 1987; the cumulative restriction map H^4(G; Z) → H^4(E; Z) ⊕ H^4(D_8; Z) ⊕ H^4(Q_8; Z) is injective. By UCT (Step 1) the same holds at U(1) coefficients. ∎

### What is *not* in the literature

- **No paper states the {E, D_8, Q_8} injectivity in our integer/U(1) form**. The closest is Quillen 1971 Theorem 5.10 (mod-2 only, elementary-abelians only) and Harada–Kono 1987 Theorem 1.1 (which decomposes the integral cohomology but does not phrase the answer as a detection theorem).
- **The "Q_8 is required" observation** is empirical (D-VAN-FIX #151). It is *consistent* with Pham 2000 (Q_8 has non-zero essential cohomology) and Adem–Karagueuzian 1997 (every-order-2-central groups are exceptional) but those papers are about a different direction of the same phenomenon. **No paper explicitly says "the detecting family for H^3(E_+(m); U(1)) must include Q_8"** — they say either (a) Q_8 is exceptional inside its own cohomology, or (b) the elementary abelian family detects H*(E_+(m); F_2).
- **No paper addresses Aut(G)-quotients of the detecting family**. Our application reduces cocycles modulo `Aut(G)` (in `modular_data.g` via `AutomorphismOrbits`). The Quillen / Harada–Kono detection statements are not Aut(G)-equivariant in print, though the conclusion goes through cleanly because both detection sources naturally factor through Aut(G).

## Open questions

1. **Does the {E, D_8, Q_8} detection extend to higher degrees?** Quillen 1971 Theorem 5.10 holds at all degrees (mod-2). Harada–Kono 1987 Theorem 1.1 gives the cyclic A(k) summand for *all* k ≡ 0 mod 4. So a degree-2j ≡ 0 mod 4 detection should follow the same pattern. **Degrees k ≢ 0 mod 4 have no cyclic summand**, so elementary abelians alone (i.e. just Quillen) suffice — no Q_8 needed. **This is the precise version of the answer to "failure modes" in #193.** At odd degrees, the question becomes whether the elementary-abelian detection is integrally exact, which is generally not stated but follows from "no torsion in those degrees" (per H–K Theorem 1.1's "A(k) = 0 if k ≢ 0 mod 4" conclusion). **TODO for the methodology paper**: do we ever need degrees other than 3 = 4 − 1 for our application? For Drinfeld doubles, no — only H^3(G; U(1)) matters. For module-category twists or higher-form symmetries we might — flagged for future work.

2. **Are there documented failures of {E, D_8, Q_8}?** Not at low degrees k ≤ 4. At very high degrees the Bockstein spectral sequence terms in Harada–Kono Lemma 3.2 and Proposition 3.1 can introduce additional structure, but the cyclic-summand picture remains. Carlson–Thévenaz 2005 §4 (Annals) supposedly handles "almost extraspecial" with the same family — TODO to acquire this paper and check for any degree-3 obstructions.

3. **Aut(G)-equivariance.** Need an explicit statement that the restriction maps res: H^3(G; U(1)) → H^3(E; U(1)) etc. are Aut(G)-equivariant. Folklore but worth a citation. Mac Lane *Homology* IV.5 covers naturality; Brown *Cohomology of Groups* §III.10 covers Aut(G) actions. No issue here; just needs writing up.

4. **UCT in dimension 3.** The UCT used to identify H^3(G; U(1)) ≅ H^4(G; Z) needs only Tor-vanishing for finite-group cohomology with divisible coefficient module; this is standard (cf. Brown *Cohomology of Groups* §III.1, Weibel §6.1). No issue.

5. **Adem–Karagueuzian acquisition.** Confirm the Theorem statement and pagination for W-1. The paper is publicly hosted at `https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~adem/essential.pdf`; acquisition is a TODO.

6. **Schuster–Yagita acquisition.** A modern restatement of Quillen 1971 Theorem 5.10 might be cleaner to cite for the methodology paper. TODO if desired.
